CH 0000Interrogator0.00 km

A telecom cable on the seafloor records earthquakes. I work on reading it.

I'm a doctoral student in the Seismology & Wave Physics group at ETH Zürich, supervised by Andreas Fichtner. My field is fibre-optic seismology: distributed acoustic sensing (DAS) turns an ordinary telecom fibre into a dense seismic array, with one channel every few metres.

I use such cables on the seafloor and under cities, and I build open tools around them: detection and picking codes for DAS, a channel-mapping method, and a literature hub for seismology.

CH 0900Beach manholehow DAS works

How one fibre becomes 1872 seismometers

  1. 01

    Light goes in

    An interrogator on land sends short laser pulses down an ordinary telecom fibre.

  2. 02

    The glass answers

    Tiny irregularities in the glass scatter a little of each pulse back, from every point along the cable.

  3. 03

    The ground writes on it

    When a wave stretches the fibre, the returning light changes. Comparing pulse with pulse gives the strain rate along the cable: here one channel every 8.17 m, 1872 channels over 15 km.

Channel number is not position

Installers store spare fibre in coils, so a fibre is longer than its route. On this cable the fibre from the interrogator to the beach manhole is 7.32 km long. The ground under it is 6.44 km. Of the difference, 0.71 km sits at eight points, in coils of 16 to 172 m.

I find them with three signals: tap tests, earthquake amplitudes and passing vehicles. The result is a position, with its uncertainty, for every channel.

CH 0925Into the seaKefalonia Transform Fault

Five templates, one cable, 17,325 earthquakes

The Kefalonia Transform Fault breaks in an earthquake above magnitude 6 every 10 to 20 years, but land networks resolve its small earthquakes poorly. In 2024 we recorded two months of DAS, from 24 April to 22 June, on a seafloor telecom cable between Kefalonia and Ithaca.

A matched filter with only five template events found 17,325 microearthquakes: nearly 20 times the 893 in the regional catalogue for the same period. They come in episodic swarms, and one cluster holds over 72 % of them. Near the template clusters, the magnitude of completeness drops by about 2 units.

  • 5template events for the matched filter
  • ≈ 2units lower magnitude of completeness, near the template clusters
