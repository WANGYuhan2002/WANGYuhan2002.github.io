What’s going on

Noise interferometry. If noise sources surround two receivers evenly, the long-time cross-correlation of their records, CAB(τ) = ⟨pA(t)·pB(t + τ)⟩, converges to the Green’s function between them (filtered by the noise spectrum): a wave as if A were a source and B a receiver, plus its time-reversed twin.

Stationary phase. Only noise sources roughly in line with A and B contribute coherently; the rest averages out. That is why one-sided noise gives a one-sided correlation. Seismologists use this to image the crust from ocean-generated noise, without any earthquakes.

Here: about 40 noise sources per second at random places in a ring around the two stations (or only on its left side), Ricker wavelets with random signs and amplitudes. Their peak frequencies vary by ±30 % to widen the noise band, and the clock runs four times faster than in the other experiments. Each 3-s window is correlated for lags of ±3 s and normalised by the energy of both records before stacking.

Equation. The 2-D constant-density acoustic wave equation ∂²p/∂t² = c(x, z)² ∇²p for the pressure p. Only P waves exist here; S and surface waves need the elastic solver of experiment 6.

Numerics. Second-order finite differences (leapfrog in time, 5-point Laplacian) on a 400 × 205 grid, computed live on your CPU (typed arrays). Courant number c·Δt/Δx ≤ 0.69 < 1/√2 and 13 grid points per dominant wavelength at v = 1. Sources are Ricker wavelets with a peak frequency of 7.6 Hz.

Units. The model is 10 km wide; v = 1 means 2.5 km/s, and a wave at v = 1 crosses the width in 4 s.
